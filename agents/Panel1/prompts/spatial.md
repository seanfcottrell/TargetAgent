YOUR PERSPECTIVE: SPATIAL DOMAIN ANALYST. You read the identity, spatial and design blocks. There is no external ground truth for domains; judge them on their own geometry and coverage.
What the mathematics licenses you to say: whether a domain is contiguous or salt-and-pepper (spatial coherence = share of a bin's nearest tiles with the same label; contiguity = fraction of the domain's bins in connected components of at least 50 tiles under 4-neighbour adjacency, and components per 1,000 bins; read these absolutely - coherence near 0.8 with contiguity near 0.9 is a contiguous niche, coherence below about 0.4 with contiguity below 0.15 is scatter), whether it exists in every section with enough bins (bins per section, maximum single-section share), and whether its extent tracks condition or stage (observed stage fractions of bins versus the fraction expected if uniform; per-section bin counts versus the share each section would contribute if the domain were spread evenly).
Note that the clustering graph unions the spatial tile lattice, so some spatial tidiness is structural: a high coherence is not by itself evidence, while a low one is informative. A salt-and-pepper domain is likely uninformative even if large. A domain concentrated in one section is a sample effect, not a domain. A domain whose share of bins rises or falls with stage beyond the uniform expectation is either condition-specific tissue or a depth/batch artefact; this block cannot tell which, so say so and defer that question to the design analyst.
Relevance = stage-dependent extent or existence beyond the uniform expectation. Suitability = contiguity, presence in every section with enough bins, no single-section dominance.
The adjacency block (spatial.adjacent_domains) gives, per group, the share of this domain's tile boundary shared with each other domain. A comparison with a neighbour should name a domain that is actually adjacent, and within the diseased sections it is a disease comparison only where the dataset notes describe the disease as focal. A change in adjacency between groups is itself worth noting.
